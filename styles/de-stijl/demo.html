De Stijl · Leiden · 1917

De
Stijl

The most restrictive visual language ever codified. Five values: red, yellow, blue, black, white. Horizontal and vertical lines only. Pure rectangles. Mathematical balance through asymmetry. Mondrian called it Neoplasticism. It is not decoration — it is philosophy.

01

Color Palette

Five values. Red, yellow, blue, black, white. No gray, no mix, no tint. These are not colors — they are philosophical positions. Each carries specific semantic weight: #e8261d for primary action, #1a4a8a for secondary, #f7c600 for tertiary. Black is structure. White is silence.

02

Typography Scale

Helvetica Neue in standard weight. De Stijl's typography was functional, not decorative. Only Oswald for display — its condensed, vertical form echoes the black lines of the grid. Never italic, never serif. Uppercase carries the movement's authority and removes individual flourish.

Buttons

Primary colors on black-bordered rectangles. Red = primary action. Blue = secondary. Yellow = tertiary. The 4px offset shadow is the only depth this style allows — it shifts on hover and collapses on press, a physical stamp interaction. No other shadow is permitted.

Form Inputs

Forms inherit the grid. border: 2px solid #1a1a1a on every input. No radius, no shadow, no soft edges. Oswald labels in uppercase. Red focus state. Checkboxes are hard squares — filled red when checked. Toggles are rectangles with no round ends, ever.

Cards

Cards are Mondrian panels. Each thumbnail is a unique composition of primary rectangles divided by black lines — no images, no gradients, no illustrations. The hover offset 5px 5px 0 #1a1a1a gives physical presence. They stamp into the page, not float above it.
